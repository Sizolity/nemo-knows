## Summary

Ishmael, seeking to cure his “hypos,” chooses a whaling voyage out of Nantucket. In New Bedford he shares a room at the Spouter‑Inn with the tattooed harpooneer Queequeg, a Polynesian royal who quickly becomes his friend. Together they sign aboard the antique, bone‑ornamented *Pequod* under the lay system, despite opaque warnings from a scarred stranger named Elijah. The ship’s owners, the “fighting Quakers” Peleg and Bildad, reveal that Captain Ahab—who lost a leg to a monstrous white whale—remains hidden below.

At sea Ahab finally emerges, branded and sleepless, and after a surreal confrontation with Stubb he casts away his pipe and calls the crew to hunt a white whale. On the quarter‑deck he nails a gold doubloon as a reward, identifies Moby Dick as his maimer, and binds the company in a quasi‑sacramental oath. Starbuck protests that vengeance on a brute is blasphemy, but Ahab is driven by a monomania that sees the whale as the “pasteboard mask” behind all cosmic malice.

The narrative then expands into an encyclopedia of cetological, philosophical, and metafictional essays. Ishmael offers his own Bibliographical classification of whales, conceding its inadequacy; argues for whaling’s dignity as exploration and industry; and meditates on the terror of whiteness, the fatal entanglement of the whale‑line, and the impossibility of accurately representing the living whale. The *Town‑Ho* inset tale delivers an early appearance of Moby Dick, who crushes the vindictive mate Radney. Encounters with other ships—the ghostly *Albatross*, the plague‑ridden *Jeroboam* with its Shaker prophet Gabriel, the oil‑begging *Jungfrau*, and the gullible French *Rose‑Bud*—inject warnings, comedy, and stinging critiques of greed.

Whaling practice is detailed through extended set‑pieces: a chase, a killing, a cutting‑in, the rendering of a sperm‑whale, and the extraction of precious ambergris. Economic logic is laid bare by the Fast‑Fish/Loose‑Fish code and Stubb’s cost‑benefit refusal to rescue the young ship‑keeper Pip. Queequeg’s obstetrical rescue of Tashtego from a spermaceti case underlines a theme of precarious physical interdependence, while the monkey‑rope literally ties together Ishmael and Queequeg. Within the Grand Armada, the *Pequod’s* boat drifts into a calm nursery of nursing whales, briefly revealing maternal tranquility before a maddened animal sends the herd into panic.

As the section closes, Pip—having jumped twice from Stubb’s boat and been left behind—is abandoned in the open sea. His exposure to the “heartless immensity” transforms him into an emblem of existential loneliness, foreshadowing the ultimate catastrophe that the retrieved text does not reach.

## Key Claims

- **Ahab’s monomania**: Ahab’s obsession congealed during his long convalescence; his sanity serves his madness. He aims vengeance not at a brute but at the inscrutable power behind all things, embodied in the white whale.
- **Moby Dick as cosmic agent**: The whale is a singular, recognisable sperm whale (snow‑white brow, crooked jaw, three holes in the starboard fluke) credited with intelligent malignity, ubiquity, and rumoured immortality.
- **The terror of whiteness**: White, divorced from kindliness, intensifies dread and points to the “heartless voids and immensities of the universe,” acting as a “colorless all‑color of atheism.”
- **The impossibility of knowledge and representation**: No picture or classification ever captures the living whale. Cetology is a “draught of a draught,” and even sworn testimony strains credibility among landsmen.
- **Fatalism and entanglement**: Men are “enveloped in whale‑lines.” The sword‑mat allegory presents necessity (warp), free will (shuttle), and chance (Queequeg’s sword). The sea is a masterless, murdering terra incognita.
- **The moral economy of whaling**: The Fast‑Fish/Loose‑Fish code distils property law to raw possession; the lay system binds men to profit; a whale’s market value can outweigh a human life, as in Stubb’s calculus with Pip.
- **The dignity and violence of the fishery**: Whalemen are explorers, liberators, and suppliers of royal anointing oil—not butchers—yet their courage coexists with casual brutality, “fighting Quaker” piety, and sultanism masked by sea‑usage.
- **Interdependence and isolation**: The monkey‑rope physically conjugates two men, illustrating a “Siamese connexion” that extends to all humanity. In contrast, Pip’s abandonment concentrates selfhood into an unmediated encounter with an indifferent void.
- **Queequeg as moral counterweight**: The “cannibal” embodies sober dignity, kindness, and an unmapped origin (“Rokovoko … not down on any map; true places never are”). His idol‑worship is treated with radical ecumenism.
- **The whale’s body as an object of wonder and paradox**: The sperm whale’s head is a boneless battering‑ram with a tiny brain; its spinal cord may be the seat of character; its spout remains a scientific riddle; foul decay yields pure spermaceti and fragrant ambergris.
