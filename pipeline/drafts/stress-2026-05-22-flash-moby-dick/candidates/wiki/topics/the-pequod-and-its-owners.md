# The Pequod And Its Owners

While searching Nantucket for a whaleship, Ishmael sifts through three vessels—*Devil‑Dam*, *Tit‑bit*, and *Pequod*—and settles on the *Pequod*, whose name recalls an extinct Massachusetts tribe. The ship itself is a composite of deep age and savage adornment: her ancient hull and worn decks carry the scars of more than half a century of whaling, and her unpanelled bulwarks bristle with sperm‑whale teeth used as belaying pins. Much of her baroque character was layered on by Captain Peleg during his years as chief mate before he retired from the sea and became one of the ship’s principal owners.

Together with his partner Bildad, Peleg embodies the type of the “fighting Quaker”—men who balance a profession of piety with an exacting commercial sense. It is this pair who interview Ishmael and Queequeg, negotiate the lay shares that bind the crew to the voyage’s profit, and disclose that the *Pequod*’s hidden captain is none other than [[ahab-and-the-white-whale|Captain Ahab]], still recovering from the loss of his leg to a monstrous white whale. Ishmael and [[queequeg-and-cross-cultural-bond|Queequeg]] sign aboard under the [[whaling-customs-and-law|lay system]], unaware that the ship’s seasoned owners have quietly set them on course toward a far grimmer purpose than the commercial hunt they profess.
