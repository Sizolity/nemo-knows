## Repeated Or Central Claims
- **Dignity of whaling**: Whaling is unjustly scorned yet historically vital (exploration, economics, light‑bearing), and whalemen provide royal anointing oil (sperm oil in coronations). The whale‑ship discovered new lands and carried missionaries.
- **The sea as truth, land as peril**: Land represents safety and spiritual slavery; the open ocean is where “landlessness alone resides highest truth.” The lee shore paradox recurs in Ishmael’s meditations.
- **Queequeg’s nobility**: Despite his “cannibal” origin, Queequeg possesses innate dignity, a “simple honest heart,” and a joint‑stock philosophy. His cross‑cultural friendship with Ishmael overcomes initial prejudice.
- **Religious tolerance and its limits**: Ishmael reasons that doing God’s will (golden rule) justifies joining Queequeg’s idol‑worship, but later dismisses Ramadan as harmful nonsense. Father Mapple’s sermon makes Jonah a model of repentance and a warning against fleeing divine truth.

- **The crew as “Isolatoes”**: Men from disparate races and continents, united only by the keel, reflect the ship as a federated microcosm.
- **Contrast and identity**: Ishmael frequently states that things are known only through their opposites—warmth through cold, self through darkness—a recurring epistemological theme.
- **The whale’s mystery and chaos of cetology**: The Extracts are explicitly unreliable; Ishmael’s formal Cetology chapter begins by bemoaning the “impenetrable veil” and “utter confusion” of classification.

## Repeated Or Central Claims
- The sperm whale is the true “monarch of the seas”; its full life, intelligence, and true physical form cannot be captured by art or science—any systematic or pictorial representation is incomplete and often erroneous.
- Ahab’s pursuit of Moby Dick is a metaphysical rebellion against the “pasteboard mask” of visible reality; he sees the white whale as either the agent of an inscrutable malice or a blank wall behind which nothing exists, and vows to strike through it.
- The crew is ritually bound to Ahab’s vengeance despite private misgivings; Starbuck calls vengeance on a “dumb brute” blasphemous, Stubb adopts fatalistic laughter, Pip appeals to a “big white God,” and Ishmael admits an involuntary sympathetic union with the feud.
- Whiteness functions as a core ambivalent symbol: it is the hue of holiness, royalty, and beauty, yet in Moby Dick it becomes a “nameless horror” that suggests absence, annihilation, and heartless voids, intensifying the whale’s terror beyond all physical danger.
- Sperm whales are depicted as intelligent and capable of deliberate, vengeful aggression; historical cases (Essex, Union, Langsdorff, Procopius, Town‑Ho) are invoked to persuade the reader of the whale’s deliberate destructiveness and support the narrative’s plausibility.
- All systems of whale knowledge are provisional and self‑conscious: the cetological “draught” is deliberately unfinished, the “Affidavit” bolsters truth with testimony, and even the best drawings (Beale, Garnery) carry admitted faults—only going whaling offers a living impression, and even that is partial.

- The sea is a “terra incognita” whose beauty conceals existential threats: the whale‑line is an “everlasting terra incognita” and a “halter round all men’s necks”; the rarely‑seen giant squid portends calamity; the desertion of small fish forebodes disaster.
