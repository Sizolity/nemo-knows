# Whiteness Motif

In *Moby-Dick*, the pallor of the white whale produces a mystical horror that outstrips ordinary dread. [[captain-ahab|Captain Ahab]]’s fixation is bound up in this hue, which the narrator Ishmael examines in detail. Ishmael catalogs the color’s many benign associations—innocence, majesty, justice—but contends that when whiteness is separated from such contexts and linked to something terrible, it intensifies the terror to its utmost limit. The ghastly pallor of the polar bear and the white shark serves as evidence. This quality, an elusive, nameless panic, underlies the novel’s symbolic architecture and turns the whale into a spectral, malevolent presence.
