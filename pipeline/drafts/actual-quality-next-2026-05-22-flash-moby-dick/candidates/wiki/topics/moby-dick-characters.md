# Moby Dick Characters

The *Pequod’s* crew gathers a cross‑section of the world’s whaling ports, from Yankee Quakers to a Pacific island cannibal, each drawn into Captain Ahab’s singular purpose.
Ishmael sails not for money but for the “idea of the whale,” a man fleeing the hypos who finds unlikely fellowship with the tattooed harpooneer Queequeg.
Queequeg, a prince among his own people, becomes Ishmael’s boon companion and, in the quarter‑deck scene, joins Tashtego and Daggoo in identifying the white whale from the twisted harpoons and peculiar spout they remember.

Ahab himself is the scarred Quaker‑captain whose ivory leg and livid “crucifixion in his face” precede his hunt for Moby Dick.
His monomania, detailed in [[ahab-vengeance-and-the-white-whale]], remains coldly rational in method even as he nails the gold doubloon to the mast and extracts a murderous oath.
Starbuck, the prudent chief mate, silently opposes the quest as a sin; Stubb responds with fatalistic humor, and Flask with blunt pugnacity.
The Parsee Fedallah leads a hidden, spectral boat crew that shadows Ahab as an omen.

Pip, the cabin‑boy turned ship‑keeper, twice abandoned in panic, drifts alone until his mind unravels—a foretaste of the voyage’s human cost cut short when the source file truncates his rescue ([[pip-the-castaway]]).
Together, these figures bind the *Pequod* into a single, perilous loom of necessity, chance, and free will.
