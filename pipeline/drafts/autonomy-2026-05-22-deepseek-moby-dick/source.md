## Summary
After the thematic prefatory apparatus—an etymology of “whale” and a wide‑ranging anthology of whale‑lore—the narrator Ishmael describes going to sea as a remedy for melancholy. In New Bedford he shares a bed with the tattooed harpooneer Queequeg, a South Sea islander and former cannibal, and the two become intimate friends. They proceed to Nantucket, where Ishmael signs aboard the weathered whaler *Pequod* under the Quaker owners Peleg and Bildad, who speak evasively of Captain Ahab, a grim one‑legged man maimed by a sperm whale. The prophet‑figure Elijah delivers cryptic warnings. The ship departs; Ishmael defends whaling’s dignity, introduces the mates (Starbuck, Stubb, Flask) and harpooneers (Queequeg, Tashtego, Daggoo), and finally presents Ahab: a scarred, ivory‑legged figure who throws his pipe into the sea and orders the masthead to cry out for a white whale.

Ahab later binds the crew on the quarter‑deck with a dramatic oath to hunt Moby Dick, revealing a hidden boat crew led by the Parsee Fedallah. Meditations on whiteness, cetacean taxonomy, and the perils of the mast‑head alternate with whaling action: the *Goney* gam, the Town‑Ho’s mutiny and Moby Dick’s lethal appearance, Stubb’s comic supper and shark‑sermon, the anatomy and cutting‑in of sperm whales, the plague‑stricken *Jeroboam* and its Shaker prophet Gabriel, a race for oil with the *Jungfrau*, the immense Grand Armada herd, Stubb’s ambergris trick on the French *Bouton de Rose*, and the precarious monkey‑rope tying Ishmael to Queequeg. The text breaks off as Pip, the genial ship‑keeper, is deliberately left to drown after a second jump from a whaleboat.

## Key Claims
- The whale is a creature of immense cultural, historical, and symbolic weight, attested by sources from Genesis to Cuvier; whaling is a dignified enterprise that opened colonies and expanded empire.
- Ishmael goes to sea as a philosophical alternative to suicidal despair, regarding the water‑gazing instinct as universal.
- Queequeg embodies the paradoxical “noble savage”: a cannibal and idolater who shows innate politeness and deep friendship, prompting Ishmael to re‑evaluate religious and cultural prejudice.
- The Nantucket Quaker whale‑owners Peleg and Bildad compartmentalize pious non‑violence toward humans with brutal violence toward whales and rigid business dealings.
- Captain Ahab is introduced as an ominous, monomaniacal figure, obsessed from his first appearance with the white whale Moby Dick, whom he views as a malevolent mask behind which he must strike.
- The sperm whale is the monarch of the seas; its life remains largely unwritten, and existing cetology is chaotic and parodied by Ishmael’s Folio/Octavo/Duodecimo classification.
- Whiteness, attached to a terrible object, induces a horror of absence and annihilation, a “colourless, all‑colour of atheism.”
- Whaling is simultaneously a brutal industrial process, a demystified craft, and a source of mystical symbolism—the whale’s brow is an unreadable Sphinx, its spout an undecidable mystery, its tail a composite of power and sensitivity.
- The unwritten laws of Fast‑Fish and Loose‑Fish govern ownership and satirize human greed and conquest.
- Supernatural fears accrete: Gabriel proclaims Moby Dick the Shaker God incarnate, Stubb suspects Fedallah is the devil and Ahab has sold his soul, and the crew senses predestined doom.
- Interdependence and mortal bonds are figured in the monkey‑rope, the ship‑board order, and fate’s warp, shuttle, and sword.
