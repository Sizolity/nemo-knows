## Repeated Or Central Claims
- **Whale as sublime and mysterious:** The “portentous and mysterious” nature of the whale motivates Ishmael’s voyage (chunks 2, 3, 4) and later fuels the cetological digression (chunk 6).
- **Nantucket as cradle of whaling:** Ishmael repeatedly insists on Nantucket’s primacy, calling it the “great original,” the “Tyre of this Carthage” relative to New Bedford (chunks 2, 4).
- **Quaker whalemen (“fighting Quakers”):** Peleg and Bildad embody a form of Quakerism that is outwardly pacifist yet sanguinary at sea; their piety mixes with sharp commercial sense and violent imagery (chunks 4, 5, 6).
- **Ishmael’s defence of whaling:** The industry is unjustly scorned; it opened the Pacific, Australia, and Japan; it supports a fleet of 700 ships and $7 million yearly imports; sperm oil may anoint monarchs (chunk 6).
- **Ahab’s mythos before his appearance:** He lost his leg to a “monstrousest parmacetty”; his name was a “foolish, ignorant whim” of his mad mother; a squaw named Tistig said it would prove prophetic; he was “a little out of his mind for a spell” from pain (chunks 4, 5).
- **Friendship with Queequeg breaks down prejudice:** Ishmael moves from terror to seeing “traces of a simple honest heart,” declares them married friends, and even joins in idol‑worship under a universalist rationalisation (chunks 3, 4, 5).
- **Fate and the sea‑soul:** The lee shore metaphor (chunk 6) and the reflection on Bulkington (chunks 2, 6) assert that the soul must reject land‑bound safety; “landlessness alone resides highest truth.”
- **Ahab’s obsessive focus and suffering:** His bronze‑like body, livid scar, “crucifixion” in his face, and his rejection of the pipe all foreshadow a monomaniacal pursuit of the white whale (chunk 6).
