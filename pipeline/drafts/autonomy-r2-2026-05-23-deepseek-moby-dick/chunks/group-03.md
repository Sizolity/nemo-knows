After the comic browbeating of the cook Fleece over whale-steak, the Pequod processes a kill (shark massacre, cutting-in, the stripped carcass’s “funeral”), and Ahab delivers a monologue before the severed sperm-whale head—the silent Sphynx. ## Repeated Or Central Claims
- **The whale as a biological and philosophical puzzle:** Blubber is argued to be the true skin (the isinglass layer merely “the skin of the skin”). Sperm-whale skin bears undecipherable “hieroglyphic” marks. The spout remains an ambiguous phenomenon—either pure mist or water mixed with breath—and is dangerous to touch. The whale has no voice yet produces a “strange rumble”; its face is unreadable, making physiognomy futile. The sperm whale’s true brain is tiny and far behind the massive forehead; Ishmael proposes reading character from the spinal vertebrae instead.
- **Mutual dependence and mortal peril:** The monkey-rope episode makes explicit the idea that all men are tied together in a “joint-stock company of woe.” Tashtego’s near-drowning in the spermaceti case and Queequeg’s “midwifery” rescue reinforce the motif of precarious survival and fraternal bonding.
- **Whaling as an honorable, ancient, and coded practice:** Whaling heroes include Perseus, St. George, Hercules, Vishnoo, and Jonah. The profession operates under two universal laws—Fast-Fish (connected possession) and Loose-Fish (free for anyone)—extended satirically to nations and social relations. English law (head to the King, tail to the Queen) is both cited and ridiculed.
- **Fedallah as shadow-villain and supernatural agent:** Stubb’s conversation with Flask frames Fedallah as devilish, unnaturally old, and in league with Ahab. Fedallah’s charm that a ship bearing both a sperm-whale head and a right-whale head cannot capsize drives the right-whale hunt. Though not directly on stage much in these chunks, his invisible influence colors crew speculation.
- **Foreshadowing of doom through prophecy and the Sphynx:** Ahab’s address to the silent sperm-whale head demands it speak of ocean-floor horrors. Gabriel’s prophecy that anyone hunting Moby Dick will die, already fulfilled with mate Macey, hangs over the ship. The “funeral” of the stripped carcass drifting as a fatal false shoal satirizes unexamined orthodoxy and hints at the Pequod’s future.
- **Sperm-whale herding and behavior:** Driven by persecution, sperm whales now form enormous schools for mutual protection. Harems consist of one “schoolmaster” bull and many females; young males form pugnacious “forty-barrel-bull” bands. When “gallied,” the herd forms revolving concentric circles, with a calm center occupied by nursing cows and calves.

## Important Local Details
- **Chapter 64–66 (Stubb’s supper & shark massacre):** Fleece, the old black cook, delivers a sermon to sharks. Queequeg and a seaman slaughter sharks with whaling-spades, revealing their self-devouring ferocity.
- **Cutting-in procedure:** The tackle (block, hawser, hundred-pound blubber-hook) is rigged to the main-top and windlass. Mates cut a hole above a side-fin; the crew heaves and the whale rolls as a spiral “scarf” peels the blubber. The boarding-sword severs the rising blanket-piece, which is lowered into the blubber-room.
- **The Sphynx (Chapter 70):** Ahab’s soliloquy before the severed sperm-whale head, demanding it disclose secrets of deep-sea death and the “nameless horror” it has witnessed.
- **The *Jeroboam* and Gabriel (Chapter 71):** Gabriel, a Shaker-prophet-turned-whaleman, claims the White Whale is the Shaker God incarnate, threatens plague, and foretells death for anyone who hunts it. Mate Macey’s death by Moby Dick is recounted, and Gabriel seizes Macey’s unopened letter from his wife.
- **Tashtego’s entombment in the case (Chapter 78):** While baling the spermaceti “Heidelburgh Tun,” Tashtego falls into the nearly emptied cavity. Queequeg dives, cuts a scuttle-hole through the head, and performs a “running delivery” to rescue him.
- **Comparative head anatomy:** The sperm whale’s head is “Platonian,” boneless, and a battering-ram; the right whale’s head is “Stoic,” with baleen and an enormous lower lip. Eye placement gives the sperm whale two independent fields of vision and a blind zone fore and aft.
- **The *Jungfrau* (Chapters 81–82):** German whaler “clean” (empty of oil); Captain Derick De Deer begs lamp oil, then competes in the hunt for an old, yellow, one‑finned bull whale that eventually sinks.
- **Pitchpoling:** A light pine lance is balanced upright on the palm and arced to strike the whale from a distance.
- **Whale law (Chapters 89–90):** Fast-Fish belongs to whoever is connected to it by a controllable medium; Loose-Fish is fair game for first capture. English law awards the head to the King and the tail to the Queen, with a satirical anecdote about the Duke of Wellington claiming a beach whale.
- **Ambergris (Chapters 91–92):** Stubb tricks the French captain of the *Bouton de Rose* into abandoning two dead whales, then cuts into one and extracts handfuls of fragrant ambergris from the bowel.
- **Pip’s castaway (Chapter 93):** Pip, the black ship-keeper, jumps twice from Stubb’s boat; the second time he is left adrift while other boats chase whales. The rescue by the ship itself begins but the source is truncated mid-sentence.
