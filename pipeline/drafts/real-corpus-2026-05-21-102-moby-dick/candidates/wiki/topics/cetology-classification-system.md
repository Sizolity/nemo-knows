# Cetology Classification System

In the narrative of *Moby-Dick*, the narrator Ishmael provides extensive taxonomic classifications of whales, a section known as Cetology. This inquiry challenges traditional Linnaean systems by proposing a "Bibliographical system" based on size rather than strict biological lineage. The text oscillates between these scientific digressions and philosophical reflections on the whale as a mysterious, monstrous entity that serves simultaneously as an economic resource and an agent of destruction.

The classification efforts are intertwined with themes of civilization versus savagery, where the narrator argues that moral standing is not defined by cultural origin but rather by actions and character. Through this lens, the study of whales becomes a vehicle for exploring broader questions about truth, representation, and the elusive nature of capturing a living form in static drawings or descriptions.
