# Whaling Practices

*Moby-Dick* renders a fictional but immersive account of mid‑19th‑century American sperm‑whale hunting. The narrative follows the *Pequod*’s crew through the full cycle of a whaling voyage: lowering boats, harpooning, lancing, towing the carcass, and flensing blubber on deck to try out oil. Practical details—from the mechanics of the windlass and cutting‑in tackles to the cooperative risks of the whaleboat crews—are embedded in Ishmael’s first‑person report, making the novel an incidental source for the material culture and techniques of open‑boat whaling.
